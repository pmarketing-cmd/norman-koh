Why most buyers get this wrong

The three traps I see Singapore buyers fall into, and what each one quietly costs.

You're doing the sensible things. Reading the launch reviews. Visiting the showflats. Asking friends who bought recently. And you still don't feel confident, because the information you're collecting is entry information.

The expensive part of a property decision lives at the other end: who buys it from you, at what price, and how many of them are standing there when you need to sell. That's the part I check first, and it's the part the brochure never mentions.

Two rules drive it. A support price: ask 100 owners if they'll sell below what they paid, and around 90 won't, so there's a floor under the market. And an exit pool: the number of buyers actually waiting for your unit on the day you sell. Count both before you fall for the showflat.

01 / Hype-Led

Buying because the showflat felt good.

The showflat is doing its job. The brochure is doing its job. Neither is doing yours.

What it costs: a $200K premium for a "sea view" that turns out to be a sliver between two buildings.

02 / Brochure-Driven

Trusting "convenient location, world-class facilities" without checking the data.

Every brochure says this. The URA pipeline, the transacted PSF, and the floor premium tell the real story.

What it costs: overpaying on the developer's markup that the resale market never returns.

03 / No Exit Math

Buying without knowing how you'll sell.

Most agents start with the entry. I start with the exit. Know the exit, or you don't know the deal.

What it costs: a property that's the right unit at the wrong time, locked up for 7 years waiting on a buyer.
